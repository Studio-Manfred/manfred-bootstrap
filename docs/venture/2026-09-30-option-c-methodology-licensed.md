# Option C — The Manfred way, licensed

> **Executive summary:** Codify the WoW as a body of teachable content — long-form writing, a book, cohort-based courses, certification. Author-brand play in the tradition of Basecamp's _Shape Up_, Team Topologies, Accelerate / DORA. Lowest capital requirement, slowest compound, smallest revenue ceiling (~€1–3M ARR realistic), but the highest defensibility of the three because content moats compound over decades. Best played as a **thought-leadership flywheel that feeds Option A** and legitimises Option B — not the primary bet, but the one that makes the other two more valuable.

## 1. Where we are today

- **The WoW is documented.** `manfred-bootstrap/docs/` has the ways-of-working overview, the superpowers workflow, stack-and-conventions, DS-first handoff spec. Enough material to be a book's spine already.
- **No public writing under the Manfred brand yet.** LinkedIn posts occasionally; no essays, no book, no course.
- **No teaching motion.** No cohort, no certification, no speaking circuit.
- **Author-brand primitives in place.** Jens has run consultancy for years; recognisable voice; Stockholm network.
- **Adjacent teachable moments** — every session like today's ends with a MEMORY note that could be adapted into a blog post with minimal edit.

## 2. Vision

**5 years out:** _The Manfred Way_ is the reference text for how modern product-tech teams should structure their work — cited in engineering-management courses, quoted in job postings ("familiar with the Manfred workflow a plus"), studied in a certification program that a few thousand engineers hold. Steady **€1–3M ARR** across book royalties, course revenue, certification fees, and speaking honoraria. Very small team (2–4 people, mostly production and community).

**Signal we're winning:** the book gets a second edition; job postings mention "Manfred way" or "role-based agents" as a desirable skill without our prompting; a professional community forms around the ideas (Discord, subreddit, conference track).

**Not the vision:** consulting engagements. Not building software. Option C is *only* content, teaching, and the reputation that flows from them.

## 4. Customer needs

**Buyer persona:** engineering managers, senior engineers, tech leads at product-tech companies who want to introduce better practices to their team.

**Sub-personas:**

- **The "next book" reader.** Already read _Team Topologies_, _Shape Up_, _Accelerate_. Looking for what's next.
- **The AI-integration overwhelmed.** Bought Cursor / Copilot but doesn't know how to make the team good at AI. Looking for a framework, not a tool.
- **The internal advocate.** Wants to introduce better practice to their team, needs a shareable framework their colleagues can also read.
- **The self-improver.** Individual contributor, midcareer, wants to learn practice better than what their company teaches them.

**Symptoms they describe:**
- "I want to bring modern AI practice to my team but don't know where to start."
- "Everything I read about AI in software is either too abstract (McKinsey slideware) or too tactical (tool tutorials)."
- "I loved _Team Topologies_ but it's from 2019 — what's the AI-era version?"
- "I need a book I can hand to my team so we're on the same page."

**Trigger events:**
- Promotion to engineering manager
- Team scaling up (5 → 15 engineers)
- Quarterly OKR planning
- Post-conference inspiration ("saw a talk — need something concrete to read")
- Failed AI adoption ("read _Accelerate_ years ago, need the AI update")

**What they buy from us:**
- Essays and blog posts (free — top of funnel)
- Email newsletter (free — engagement layer)
- The book (€25–35 physical, €15 digital)
- Cohort course (€800–1500 per seat, small live sessions)
- Certification (€300 individual, €5–15K team)
- Speaking fees (€3–10K per talk for corporate audiences)

**What they don't buy from us:**
- Software (that's Option B)
- Embedded delivery (that's Option A)
- 1:1 advisory (unless a very selective retainer — could be Option A tail)

## 5. Market

### Global

- **Engineering-management book market:** _Team Topologies_ sold 100,000+ copies. _Accelerate_ similar. _Shape Up_ ships free but generates massive inbound for Basecamp.
- **Cohort-course market (Reforge, Maven, Section):** €500M–1B combined, with segment for engineering/product content **~€100–200M**
- **Certification market for engineering practices:** existing programs (Scrum, SAFe, DORA) do 6-figure-annual revenue; ceiling probably 8-figure globally per successful methodology
- **AI-era methodology is greenfield.** Nobody has yet claimed the "how AI-first teams work" methodology position the way _Team Topologies_ claimed team design.

### Europe

- Strong reading + certification culture (Nordics + DACH especially)
- English content works for pro readership across Europe
- Conference circuit dense and healthy (LeadDev, KubeCon, Config, JavaScript Nation, Devoxx, EuroPython — pick your lanes)

### Sweden

- Small local market but disproportionately influential engineering community per capita (Spotify, Klarna, iZettle, Voi alumni network)
- Speaking-circuit-friendly (small enough to build reputation quickly)
- Publishing houses less established than US/UK — probably self-publish or work with US publisher

### Stockholm

- Meetups are healthy but not the primary channel; content is inherently global
- Local advantage: alumni network can be book blurb + case study source (Spotify + Klarna + Voi engineers as testimonial voices)

## 6. Risks

### Desirability

- **Methodology market is crowded.** _Team Topologies_, _Shape Up_, _Accelerate_, DORA, SPACE, SREs, _An Elegant Puzzle_, _Staff Engineer_ — all competing for the same reader's attention. **Mitigation:** the AI-first angle is a real differentiator; no existing methodology has AI as its native starting point.
- **"AI methodology" fatigue.** Every consultant is publishing AI takes. **Mitigation:** we don't publish takes; we publish practice with real code and real evidence. The reader can copy-paste our work and use it.
- **Book market is soft.** Physical book sales are down structurally. **Mitigation:** book is the credibility artifact, not the revenue driver; courses + certification are the money.

### Feasibility

- **Content production is time-heavy.** A book is 12–18 months of writing. Essays cost 1–2 days each done well. **Mitigation:** every Manfred session already produces a MEMORY note; those are the raw material — the writing is adaptation, not from scratch.
- **Slow to compound.** Content plays take 2–3 years to reach steady state. Requires patience unlike Options A / B which have signal within 6–12 months.
- **Jens-dependent by default.** Author-brands don't easily hand off. **Mitigation:** design the methodology to have named contributors (Moa, Selma, others) from day 1 — a multi-voice framework has more legs than a single-author one.

### Viability

- **Low ceiling.** Realistic top-end: **€1–3M ARR** steady state. Team of 4 max. No VC exit path.
- **High margin, low variable cost.** Course + book + cert are near-pure margin at scale.
- **Reliable but slow.** The revenue is real but takes years to build. Doesn't fund the ambitious Options A/B build-out on its own.
- **Best as a supporting play**, not the primary bet.

## 8. Brand and marketing

### Positioning

**One-liner:** _"The book, the course, and the practice for AI-first product teams."_

**Value promise:** _"Everything you need to run your team the way the best AI-first product-tech shops actually run — proven, teachable, ready to install."_

**Anti-positioning:** not a consultant. Not a platform. Not a tool. **A body of practice.**

### Voice

- Authoritative but humble — "here's what we do; here's why; try it and see"
- Story-driven — every essay opens with a real incident
- Evidence-based — every claim linked to a git commit or a ticket
- Pedagogical — the reader should feel taught, not sold to

### Channels (first 6 months)

1. **Long-form essays on the domain.** 6–8 in the first quarter. Every one publishable-quality (aim for HN front page).
2. **Email list.** Every essay drives signups. Weekly or bi-weekly cadence.
3. **LinkedIn.** Short summaries + link to essay. Aim for engineering-leader audience.
4. **Speaking.** 1 talk in Q4 2026 (small meetup — practice), 2–3 in H1 2027 (larger conferences).
5. **Book proposal circulation.** Q1 2027.
6. **Podcast appearances as a guest** — cheaper than starting our own. Target: 4–6 guest appearances in first 6 months.
7. **Twitter/X or Bluesky** — light-touch. Amplify essays; not a primary channel.

### Brand architecture

**Recommendation:** author-brand under Jens Wedin, publishing under a light content sub-brand (_The Manfred Way_ or similar). Jens's name is the reputation; the sub-brand is the artefact identifier.

Not a good fit for a product brand — content businesses run on trust between reader and author, and named-author models sell better than opaque brand models in this genre (compare Wardley → _Wardley Mapping_, or Skelton/Pais → _Team Topologies_).
