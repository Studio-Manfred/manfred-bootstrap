# Q4 2026 — the story arc, the gaps, and the experiments

> **Executive summary:** Three parallel low-cost experiments in October–December 2026, each testing exactly one of options A / B / C. By the end of Q1 2027, whichever experiment shows the strongest signal becomes the venture's lead wedge; the entity is formed against it; the other two either sunset or reshape into supporting flywheels. This document is the compass — how we sequence the year, what we don't yet know, and what we ship in the next 12 weeks to know it.

## 1. The story arc

### The next 12 weeks (Oct–Dec 2026)

Three parallel experiments — one per option — each capped at ~40 hours of Jens's time and ≤€2K in cash. See §3 for the specific ships. Purpose: **generate signal, not revenue.** Signal means "at least one credible buyer said yes with a real commitment" (paid pilot, paid preorder, paid cohort seat). Revenue is a Q1 2027 problem.

### Q1 2027 (Jan–Mar)

Read the signal. Rank the three experiments by conviction. **Pick one as the lead wedge.** Form the Swedish AB against it. Convert the best pilot / preorder / cohort into a full-price paid engagement. Kill or shelve the two weaker options — or reshape them as feeders (Option C's content feeds Option A's client pipeline; Option A's client work is Option B's R&D). Publish the first credible case study.

### 2027 (rest of year)

Scale the wedge. If it's A: 4–6 concurrent retainers by Q4, first named hire. If it's B: paid platform beta live, self-serve funnel instrumented, waitlist → paid conversion metric visible. If it's C: 3–4 cohorts delivered, book manuscript underway, speaking circuit warming.

Second option starts as a supporting R&D layer once the wedge has 6+ months of steady operating cash.

### 2028

Wedge is a real business with predictable monthly economics. Adjacent option (A→B or A→C) begins to contribute independent revenue. Possible external capital conversation IF unit economics are showing platform-scale growth — otherwise stay bootstrap-viable and profitable.

### 5 years out (end of 2031)

- **If A won:** 20–30 retainers, 5–10 team, €3–6M ARR, ~70% gross margin, high referral rate. Possibly with B contributing 20–30% of revenue as productised layer.
- **If B won:** 200–800 paying platform teams, €8–20M ARR, 15–30 team, likely Series A raised in 2027–28.
- **If C won:** €1–3M ARR from books/courses/certifications, 3–5 team, high-margin lifestyle-scale business with global reader footprint.

### What determines the chronology

- **Bias toward A first.** A funds itself immediately and creates the evidence that legitimises B and C. B without A's case studies is a pitch; A without B is a real business the day the first retainer is signed.
- **C is patience-shaped.** Content plays take 2–3 years to compound; starting the essays and email list in Q4 costs almost nothing and creates 2027 optionality.
- **B is capital-shaped.** Committing to B before we can fund it becomes a distraction; the Q4 experiment is deliberately small (a landing page + a waitlist) to test the demand hypothesis without triggering the build.

## 2. Honest gaps

For each gap: what we don't know, why it matters, and which Q4 experiment closes it.

### G1 — What are buyers actually willing to pay?

**What we don't know:** every pricing number in the option docs (€8–15K/month retainer, €49–499/team/month platform, €800–1500 cohort seat) is a research-informed guess. We haven't watched a real buyer hand over money.

**Why it matters:** the entire business case hinges on pricing tolerance. €5K/month vs. €15K/month for the same delivery is the difference between "team of 2" and "team of 6."

**Closed by:** Experiment A1 (pilot commitment) — the pilot's paid contract, even at 50% of target rate, is the first real pricing data point.

### G2 — Who's the wedge-defining first customer?

**What we don't know:** the option docs describe buyer personas but no specific named account. "VP Eng at Series A/B SaaS" is a segment, not a customer.

**Why it matters:** the first paid engagement is the shape of the next ten. If pilot #1 is a fintech, the pipeline that follows will be fintech-shaped. If it's a design-heavy consumer app, the pipeline is consumer-shaped. Choosing badly locks in a mismatch.

**Closed by:** Experiments A1 + C1 — the outreach and the essay comments both surface named prospective buyers and reveal which segment leans in hardest.

### G3 — Can a second operator deliver from the codified WoW?

**What we don't know:** whether the methodology (bootstrap + roles + DS-first + superpowers) is genuinely learnable and hand-off-able, or is silently Jens-shaped in ways that only show up when someone else tries to run an engagement.

**Why it matters:** if it's Jens-only, the ceiling is one client at a time forever. If a second operator can deliver, the scaling story is real.

**Closed by:** Experiment A1 — deliberately run part of the pilot with a second Manfred operator (Moa, Selma, or a recruit) shadowing Jens and running the second half solo. Every friction point is a follow-up ticket to codify further.

### G4 — Does the OSS/platform demand actually exist outside our head?

**What we don't know:** whether the "process discipline on top of AI dev tools" positioning resonates with anyone besides us. The market signals suggest it should — 22% of enterprises name organisational barriers as their #1 AI blocker — but we haven't tested it directly.

**Why it matters:** Option B requires €1–3M seed. If demand isn't real, that's real money and 18 months lost.

**Closed by:** Experiment B1 (public OSS release + waitlist landing) — waitlist signup rate + HN comment quality tell us whether the story lands with an audience that isn't already a Manfred fan.

### G5 — Will people pay to be taught this, or only read it?

**What we don't know:** whether the WoW is book-shaped, course-shaped, or blog-post-shaped. All three sizes exist for methodology content — but they compound differently and demand different investment.

**Why it matters:** Option C's success case is the cohort course. If people read the essays but don't pay for the course, C is a hobby (blog) not a business (school).

**Closed by:** Experiment C1 — publish the first 2–3 essays, run a "would you buy a cohort?" survey on the email list, take waitlist commitments (paid deposit €100 refundable) for a Q1 2027 cohort. Real deposits are real signal.

### G6 — Which brand do we go to market under?

**What we don't know:** whether to extend Studio Manfred, spin up a new brand, or run under Manfred with a rebrand option at Q1 2027 entity formation. Also — if new brand, what name.

**Why it matters:** every piece of marketing collateral we ship in Q4 solidifies the brand identity. A rebrand in Q1 has real cost if we've built up a domain, an email list, and case studies under one name.

**Closed by:** early. Not by an experiment — by a Q4 2026 decision. See §4.

### G7 — What's the shared-IP model with investors and stakeholders?

**What we don't know:** the mechanics of the "shared IP with investors/stakeholders" the user mentioned in the venture prompt. Is it a formal co-founder agreement, an advisor-equity model, an open-source contributor licence, or something else?

**Why it matters:** determines who has what rights to the methodology if the venture is sold, forked, or wound down. Also affects investor terms if we take external capital.

**Closed by:** Q4 legal review (fractional-legal engagement, ~4 hours). Not an experiment; a decision that unlocks the rest.

### G8 — Is Jens the CEO, the CTO, or the practice lead?

**What we don't know:** which role Jens actually wants to be in the venture. Option A is CEO/practice-lead-heavy; Option B is CTO/product-heavy; Option C is author/spokesperson-heavy.

**Why it matters:** determines what we need to hire (Option A needs a strong second operator; Option B needs product-eng leadership; Option C needs an editor and community manager). Also determines Jens's own energy allocation.

**Closed by:** self-reflection, not an experiment. This one belongs in the founder-questions round with STU-985. The three experiments are structured so that Jens plays a different role in each — noticing which one feels most sustainable is the signal.

### G9 — Financial runway

**What we don't know:** how long Jens can run at reduced income while the pilots ramp. Determines whether the accelerated capital path (€300–500K F&F) is optional or necessary.

**Why it matters:** if the wedge takes 9 months instead of 3 to hit €10K MRR, we need to know we can survive that. If we can't, we need to close the capital gap in October, not in Q1.

**Closed by:** ~2 hours with a spreadsheet. Not an experiment. Put on the Q4 decision list.

### G10 — What we DO know

Not a gap — but worth naming so we don't over-inflate the uncertainty list:

- The methodology works end-to-end (STU-980 shipped in 2.5 hours; the git log is the receipt)
- Stockholm has the capital, the talent, and the pattern (Klarna/Spotify alumni network) to support the venture
- The enterprise-AI gap is documented and quantified (22% cite "way we operate" as primary barrier)
- The pricing benchmarks are real and public — hybrid retainer is the emerging norm
- No obvious direct competitor holds the "AI product ops as a service" position in Stockholm

The knowns outnumber the unknowns.

## 3. Q4 2026 experiment slate

Three experiments, one per option, running in parallel. Each has a hypothesis, a specific ship, a success signal, a cost cap, the gap(s) it closes, and kill criteria.

### Experiment A1 — Deliver a paid pilot to one real client

**Hypothesis:** a Series-A-to-C product-tech company will pay ≥€5K for a 4-week embed that installs the Manfred way in their team and demonstrably improves ship rate.

**What we ship (October):**
- Landing-page-lite (single page under manfred.se or jenswedin.com) describing the offer, the evidence, and the price
- Warm-outreach list of 20 prospects (existing network — Manfred alumni, VC portfolio companies where warm intros exist)
- Discovery-call script + pilot proposal template
- Pilot engagement contract (fractional-legal-reviewed)

**What we ship (November):**
- Signed pilot #1 (€5K, 4-week embed)
- Weekly ledger + baseline ship-rate measurement per the WoW
- Half-way review with client stakeholder
- Second pilot signed if pipeline supports

**What we ship (December):**
- Pilot #1 delivered end-to-end
- First case study drafted (client-approved)
- Retainer proposal for month 5+ delivered to client

**Success signal:**
- **Green:** pilot #1 signed, delivered, client agrees to retainer or a second engagement
- **Yellow:** pilot signed and delivered but client won't convert to retainer (still valuable — you get the case study and the pricing data point)
- **Red:** no pilot signed by end of November despite 20+ outreach conversations

**Cost cap:** ~40 hours of Jens's time (mostly discovery calls, pilot delivery, and writing) + ~€2K cash (legal review of contract, minimal marketing collateral, travel to two onsite meetings)

**Gaps closed:** G1 (pricing), G2 (buyer persona), G3 (second-operator handoff if we deliberately shadow), G8 (Jens-as-practice-lead feel test)

**Kill criteria:** if no pilot signs by 30 November and the outreach conversations reveal a consistent objection ("we already bought X"; "we don't hire embedded consultants"; "price is too high for the scope"), Option A is not the wedge for 2026. Pivot to B or C for the Q1 2027 relaunch.

### Experiment B1 — Public OSS release + waitlist for a hosted platform

**Hypothesis:** if we open-source `manfred-bootstrap` under a permissive licence and publish a waitlist for a hosted platform tier, we get ≥300 GitHub stars and ≥200 waitlist signups in the first 8 weeks, with a mix of buyer personas (not just Manfred fans).

**What we ship (October):**
- Public `manfred-bootstrap` repo (relicensed MIT/Apache, secrets scrubbed, README rewritten for external audience)
- Landing page (one page) — "The way modern product-tech teams work. Early access Q1 2027." with an email signup
- HN launch post drafted and posted (weekday morning US-East for max engagement)
- 2 launch essays under Manfred domain, cross-posted to dev.to / hashnode

**What we ship (November):**
- Second HN post (a specific technical deep-dive from the DS-first arc)
- 5 more community-directed essays on subdomains of the WoW (role registry, DS-first, superpowers)
- Weekly changelog on the domain

**What we ship (December):**
- Analytics on waitlist signups (which channels? which segments?)
- 1:1 conversations with 10 highest-signal waitlist signups (unpaid — discovery only)
- Decision-ready doc: "should we build the platform in 2027?"

**Success signal:**
- **Green:** 500+ GitHub stars, 400+ waitlist signups, ≥10 signals-from-a-non-Manfred-fan among the top 20 (measured by intake survey field: "how did you find us?")
- **Yellow:** 200–500 stars, 200–400 waitlist — real demand exists but soft, hold decision to Q2 2027
- **Red:** <100 stars, <100 waitlist — either the story doesn't land or the distribution wasn't good enough; investigate and defer

**Cost cap:** ~30 hours of Jens's time + ~€500 cash (hosting for landing page, HN promotion is free, essay publishing is free)

**Gaps closed:** G4 (does demand exist), G2 (waitlist survey reveals buyer segment), G6 (community feedback on brand naming)

**Kill criteria:** if signups stall at <100 and HN doesn't front-page either launch, Option B's demand hypothesis is unproven. Don't invest platform-build capital in 2027 — either revisit the pitch or shelve.

### Experiment C1 — Publish essays, launch email list, take cohort deposits

**Hypothesis:** the WoW has enough public readership that a €100-refundable-deposit for a Q1 2027 cohort course will attract ≥30 signups within 8 weeks of the first essay.

**What we ship (October):**
- Ship 2 essays under Manfred domain (or a new subdomain — e.g., manfredway.com or thermanfredway.com — decision needed by mid-October per G6)
- Launch email list (Ghost or Substack; recommend Ghost)
- Post essays on LinkedIn + newsletter + submit to HN

**What we ship (November):**
- 2 more essays (total 4)
- Email list to 300+ subscribers
- Announce "Q1 2027 cohort — reserve seat with €100 deposit" call-to-action in the newsletter

**What we ship (December):**
- 2 more essays (total 6)
- Deposit count as the primary success metric
- First guest-authored piece from Moa or Selma (multi-voice signal for later book)

**Success signal:**
- **Green:** 30+ paid deposits (€3K+ collected), 1500+ email list, 3+ essays hit HN front page
- **Yellow:** 10–30 deposits, 800–1500 list — real demand but slow; run a smaller cohort at Q1 2027 or bundle with A pilots
- **Red:** <10 deposits despite 1500+ subscribers — people read but don't pay; C is a blog not a business

**Cost cap:** ~25 hours of Jens's time (writing + light promotion) + ~€200 cash (email tool, domain if new brand)

**Gaps closed:** G5 (will people pay), G2 (essay comments reveal buyer profile), G6 (naming decision if new brand), G8 (Jens-as-author feel test)

**Kill criteria:** if essays don't get read (< 500 reads each) despite consistent shipping, the positioning is off — retest with different framing. If they get read but zero deposits, C is not a business; keep the newsletter as marketing for A / B.

## 4. The Q1 2027 decision framework

At end of December 2026, we have three signal reports. The decision heuristic:

**Step 1 — Grade each experiment red / yellow / green** per its own success criteria (see §3).

**Step 2 — Apply the tiebreakers:**

| Situation | Recommended wedge |
|---|---|
| A green, B/C any | **A** (the strongest signal wins by default; A also has revenue attached) |
| A yellow, B green | Watch closely; if B signup rate is accelerating in December, consider **B** as the wedge with A as pilot delivery |
| A yellow, C green | **A + C combined** — deliver pilots at reduced rate as "case-study partnerships"; use C's audience as pipeline |
| All red | Serious retrospective. Options: reshape the pitch and try again in Q1; take a founder sabbatical; abandon the venture and return to Studio Manfred client work |
| A red, B green, C green | **B or C** as wedge; suggests methodology is teachable/lovable but not embed-shaped |

**Step 3 — Entity formation.** Whichever wedge wins, we form the AB in January against it. If A, entity is delivery-first; if B, entity is product-first; if C, entity is publishing-first. Structure differences are real (option pool, IP terms, board composition) so this isn't a paperwork step — it's a signal-driven choice.

**Step 4 — Publish the wedge story publicly.** By end of January, one blog post: "We ran three experiments. Here's what we learned. Here's what we're building." This becomes the founding narrative and the first artifact new hires / stakeholders / press read.

**Step 5 — First hire.** By end of Q1 2027: one named collaborator committed part-time or full-time. Which discipline depends on the wedge (see G8).
